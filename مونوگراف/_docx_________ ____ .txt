[Heading 1] 1.1 Background
[Normal (Web)] Educational institutions worldwide are increasingly adopting information and communication technologies (ICT) to manage academic and administrative work. Private academies, in particular, depend on timely access to student records, attendance data, and financial information to coordinate daily operations. Where processes remain paper-based or spread across separate files, staff spend disproportionate time on data entry and reconciliation rather than on teaching support,
[Normal (Web)] In contexts where digital infrastructure is still developing, the gap between institutional needs and available tools is often wider. Schools and academies may use spreadsheets, messaging applications, or partial software solutions that do not share a common database. Learning analytics research shows that when educational data are collected systematically, institutions can monitor student progress more accurately and respond to academic difficulties at an earlier stage ,
[Normal (Web)] Manual and fragmented systems also weaken internal communication. Announcements, schedule changes, and performance feedback reach students and teachers through informal channels, which increases the likelihood of missed or inconsistent information. Studies on technology-mediated education confirm that integrated platforms improve coordination among learners, instructors, and administrators ,
[Normal (Web)] Recent advances in web and mobile development have made it feasible to build unified academic systems at lower cost. Server-side frameworks such as Node.js support concurrent requests from multiple users, while React and Flutter allow responsive web and cross-platform mobile interfaces,
[Normal (Web)] Document-oriented databases such as MongoDB accommodate evolving record structures without frequent schema migrations, which suits institutions whose requirements change over time ,
[Normal (Web)] Educational data mining and performance analytics have further extended what management systems can offer beyond basic record keeping. By examining attendance trends, assessment results, and engagement indicators, institutions can identify students who may need additional support and adjust instructional strategies accordingly,
[Normal (Web)] Nokta Academy currently lacks such a unified environment. The proposed Nokta Academy Management System (NAMS) is intended to combine administrative automation, secure data management, and analytics within a single web-mobile platform tailored to the academy's operational context.

[Heading 1] 1.4 Scope and Limitations
[Normal (Web)] The scope of this project defines what NAMS will include during design, implementation, and evaluation. It follows directly from the objectives and research questions stated in Sections 1.3 and 1.3.3 and reflects the operational needs of Nokta Academy.
[Heading 1] 1.4.1 Scope (Inclusions)
[Normal (Web)] Features: The system will include user authentication and role-based access; student registration and profile management; attendance recording and reporting; grade entry and transcript generation; class and timetable scheduling; fee and payment tracking; institutional announcements and notifications; administrative dashboards; and performance analytics based on collected academic data.
[Normal (Web)] Platforms: NAMS will be delivered as a web application for administrators and academic staff (React), a cross-platform mobile application for students and teachers (Flutter for Android and iOS), and a RESTful backend API (Node.js with Express.js) connected to a Mongo Data Base (DB) database.
[Normal (Web)] Users: Primary users are academy administrators, teachers, and enrolled students. Each role will access functions appropriate to its responsibilities, such as record management for administrators, grade and attendance entry for teachers, and schedule and result viewing for students.
[Normal (Web)] Data: The system will manage structured records for an estimated 500 to 1,000 active users, including student biodata, attendance logs, assessment results, financial transactions, timetables, and communication history. Data will be stored centrally to support reporting and analytics within the academy.
[Heading 1] 1.4.2 Limitations (Exclusions)
[Normal (Web)] Geographic: The study and deployment scope are limited to Nokta Academy. The system is not designed for multi-campus operation, national education networks, or integration with external government examination or certification authorities.
[Normal (Web)] Technical: The project will not implement a full learning management system with video conferencing, online examination proctoring, or offline-first synchronization. Analytics will rely on rule-based and statistical methods rather than advanced machine learning models. Third-party payment gateway integration and biometric authentication are excluded from the initial prototype.
[Normal (Web)] Resource: Development is constrained by the duration of a final-year bachelor project, limited team size, and restricted budget for infrastructure and external services. User acceptance testing will be conducted with a sample of academy staff and students rather than a large-scale longitudinal study.

[Normal] Academy management systems are digital information systems designed to support academic administration and institutional operations within educational academies and private learning centers. These systems typically manage core functions such as student enrollment, attendance tracking, examinations, scheduling, financial records, communication, and academic reporting through centralized data environments. Research in educational information systems indicates that the use of digital platforms reduces manual workload, minimizes data duplication, and improves the accuracy and availability of institutional information ,
[Normal] Modern academy management systems are increasingly designed as integrated platforms that combine academic and administrative functions within a single environment. Instead of separating learning-related activities from administrative processes, these systems provide unified access to communication tools, scheduling systems, reporting dashboards, and academic records. This integration supports improved coordination between students, teachers, and administrators and enhances institutional decision-making through real-time data access
[Normal] Web-based dashboards represent a fundamental component of contemporary academy management systems. These dashboards provide centralized interfaces for administrators and academic staff to manage student records, attendance, grading, financial operations, and institutional reporting. Studies show that web-based administrative systems improve operational efficiency and enable better data-driven decision-making by providing structured and real-time access to academic information,
[Normal] Mobile applications have also become an essential part of modern academy management environments. Students, teachers, and parents increasingly rely on mobile platforms to access schedules, notifications, attendance records, and academic results. Research highlights that mobile accessibility improves user engagement and enhances communication between stakeholders in educational environments. However, usability issues, performance limitations, and lack of offline functionality may negatively affect system adoption and user satisfaction ,
[Normal] Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) is widely implemented in academy management systems to ensure secure and structured access to sensitive data. In educational environments, different user roles such as administrators, teachers, students, and parents require different levels of system permissions. RBAC improves system security by restricting unauthorized access to academic, financial, and personal data. This approach is essential in protecting sensitive institutional records such as examination results, attendance data, and financial transactions,
[Normal] In addition to RBAC, secure authentication mechanisms are critical in protecting academy management systems. These mechanisms include password encryption, token-based authentication, session management, and audit logging. Research shows that weak authentication systems increase vulnerability to unauthorized access and data breaches, which can reduce user trust and compromise institutional integrity,
[Normal] Modern academy management systems are typically built using modular and scalable system architectures that support integration between web applications, mobile applications, and centralized databases. This architectural approach enables flexibility, easier maintenance, and future system expansion. Modular design is particularly important in educational environments where system requirements evolve over time to include additional functionalities such as analytics, communication tools, and financial modules (Tilkov et al., 2010).
[Normal] Database systems form the backbone of academy management platforms by storing and organizing large volumes of academic and administrative data. These systems manage student records, attendance logs, financial transactions, examination results, and communication data in a structured manner. Research indicates that well-designed database architectures improve system performance, data consistency, and long-term scalability in educational environments (Chodorow, 2013).
[Normal] Multilingual support is an important feature in academy management systems, particularly in regions with diverse language preferences. These systems must accommodate users who speak different languages such as local and international languages to ensure accessibility and effective communication. Studies show that multilingual interfaces enhance usability and improve user engagement by reducing language barriers in digital educational platforms,
[Normal] Offline functionality is also an important consideration in academy management systems, especially in areas with unstable internet connectivity. Offline support enables users to access essential academic data such as schedules, attendance, and notifications even without internet access. However, offline systems must include synchronization mechanisms to ensure data consistency and prevent conflicts when reconnecting to the server,
[Normal] Therefore, modern academy management systems require integrated solutions that support academic administration, secure access control, multilingual communication, mobile accessibility, and reliable data management. These requirements align with the design objectives of the Nokta Academy Management System (NAMS), which aims to provide a centralized, secure, and scalable platform for academic and administrative operations.
[Normal] The development and adoption of academy management systems can be explained through established technology acceptance and information systems success theories. These frameworks provide a theoretical basis for understanding how users adopt and interact with digital academic platforms, particularly in educational environments where multiple user groups with different needs are involved.
[Normal] One of the most widely used models in technology adoption research is the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology 2 (UTAUT2). This model explains user adoption behavior through factors such as performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, facilitating conditions, hedonic motivation, price value, and habit,
[Normal] In the context of academy management systems, these factors determine whether students, teachers, parents, and administrative staff accept and continuously use digital platforms. Systems that improve usability, accessibility, communication efficiency, and operational convenience are more likely to achieve higher adoption rates. The design of the Nokta Academy Management System (NAMS), particularly its mobile accessibility and multilingual support, aligns with key principles of UTAUT2 by enhancing perceived usefulness and ease of use for different user groups.
[Normal] Another important theoretical foundation is the DeLone and McLean Information Systems Success Model. This model evaluates system success based on six key dimensions: system quality, information quality, service quality, use, user satisfaction, and net organizational benefits, .
[Normal] In academy environments, these dimensions are critical for assessing whether a management system effectively supports academic operations. For instance, reliable reporting mechanisms, accurate academic records, secure access control, and user-friendly interfaces contribute directly to user satisfaction and institutional efficiency.
[Normal] In addition, digital transformation theory provides a broader conceptual perspective for understanding the evolution of academy management systems. Digital transformation emphasizes the integration of digital technologies into organizational processes to improve efficiency, service delivery, and decision-making,
[Normal] In educational institutions, this transformation shifts traditional manual and fragmented administrative processes toward integrated, data-driven, and user-centered digital platforms. The NAMS framework reflects this transformation by replacing disconnected academic and administrative processes with a unified system.
[Normal] Furthermore, research on digital learning adoption highlights the importance of usability-related factors such as perceived usefulness, ease of use, compatibility, mobility, and system quality in influencing user acceptance of educational technologies ,
[Normal] These factors are particularly relevant to NAMS because the system is designed to support continuous access for students, teachers, and parents through both web and mobile platforms.
[Normal] Collectively, these theoretical perspectives provide a strong foundation for the design and evaluation of NAMS by emphasizing user acceptance, system quality, and digital transformation in educational environments.

Existing systems often address these features individually rather than integrating them into a single framework. As a result, private academies operating in multilingual and connectivity-constrained environments face usability and accessibility challenges that reduce system effectiveness and user satisfaction (Al-Emran et al., 2018; Al-Maroof et al., 2021).
[Normal] The literature also highlights a gap in integrated web–mobile ERP-style architectures for academy environments. While some systems provide administrative dashboards and others offer mobile learning applications, few platforms successfully integrate secure academic management, financial operations, communication tools, and mobile accessibility in a consistent and synchronized environment. This separation often leads to system fragmentation and reduces operational efficiency (Rahman & Saha, 2019).
[Normal] Furthermore, there is a noticeable gap between institutional-level systems and user-centered design approaches. Many existing academy platforms are designed primarily for administrative use and provide limited interactive functionality for students, teachers, and parents. This creates a usability gap, as modern users increasingly expect real-time access to academic information and services through mobile devices and intuitive interfaces (Traxler, 2018; Al-Emran et al., 2018).
[Normal] Language accessibility remains another under-addressed issue in many academy systems. A significant number of existing platforms are developed primarily for English-speaking environments and do not adequately support multilingual user bases. This limitation can hinder communication and reduce accessibility for stakeholders in diverse linguistic contexts. Research suggests that multilingual support significantly improves usability, engagement, and communication efficiency in educational systems (Al-Maroof et al., 2021).
[Normal] In addition, unstable internet connectivity continues to be a practical challenge in many private academies, particularly in developing regions. Interruptions in network availability can negatively impact attendance tracking, communication, and access to academic resources. Although some systems provide online functionality, limited offline support remains a key gap in ensuring continuous system usability under unstable network conditions (Traxler, 2018; Al-Emran et al., 2018).
[Normal] Another important gap identified in system development research is the lack of clear distinction between implemented features and future enhancements in many academic system studies. Without this distinction, it becomes difficult to accurately evaluate system maturity and practical applicability. Therefore, transparent classification of system components is essential for improving research reliability and clarity (Peffers et al., 2012).
[Normal] Overall, the literature suggests a strong need for integrated academy management systems that combine administrative efficiency, secure role-based access, multilingual interfaces, mobile accessibility, reporting and analytics, and operational stability under limited connectivity. These requirements highlight the necessity of unified digital platforms that move beyond isolated learning or administrative tools. In response to these identified gaps, the Nokta Academy Management System (NAMS) has been designed as an integrated solution that supports academic management, communication, multilingual interaction, secure access control, and structured reporting within a single cohesive platform.
